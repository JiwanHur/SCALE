The idea

Learn the guidance
during training.

Classifier-free guidance improves generation quality, but requires additional model evaluations at sampling time. Existing guidance-free methods learn to approximate that signal.

We introduce self-contrastive guidance (SCG): contrast the model’s predictions on real and self-generated samples to obtain a signal of its own prediction errors. SCALE incorporates this signal into the likelihood training objective, so the model produces enhanced predictions with one model evaluation per sampling step.

The same framework applies to diffusion and autoregressive models, and complements classifier-free guidance and preference-based fine-tuning.

How SCALE works

A contrast at training time.
A single prediction at sampling time.

  1. 01

    Generate a reference

    Use the model’s own prediction to construct a self-generated sample paired with a real training sample.

  2. 02

    Learn from the contrast

    Reparameterize the likelihood objective to absorb the difference between real and reference predictions into the model.

  3. 03

    Sample without guidance

    Use a single model evaluation per step, with the learned conditioning parameter controlling the implicit guidance strength.

PatchMix keeps the reference closer to the data.

Mixing patches from real and self-generated samples provides a more reliable guidance anchor when generated samples are degraded. We use a mixing ratio of λ = 0.2 across our experiments.
